# Green Computing

_This is a draft. More info with respect to Green Compute and Green Disc applications to come early 2027_

## Why it matters

Digital research has a real carbon footprint — but most of the steps that reduce it also make your group's work faster, cheaper, and easier to reproduce. This isn't about extra overhead; it's mostly good research practice reframed.

In practical terms, greener computing tends to mean:
- **Lower costs** — cloud and compute bills scale with usage; efficiency pays directly
- **Faster results** — leaner workflows spend less time running (and less time waiting in queues)
- **Less time lost** — good data hygiene means less time hunting for files or re-running analyses from scratch
- **Stronger grant applications** — funders are increasingly asking about environmental impact
- **Formal recognition** — Green DiSC accreditation is something your group can cite

## Practical steps

### Run analyses more efficiently
Efficient workflows save time and money, and happen to be greener too.
- Test and debug on small inputs before running at full scale — catching problems early avoids costly full reruns.
- Use workflow managers to manage jobs, storing and cleaning intermediate files according to the needs of thejob.
- If you're using UCL HPCs, cloud or paid compute, request only the resources you actually need — over-provisioning is a common and unnecessary cost.

### Keep data under control
Storage costs money and energy, and unmanaged data creates headaches for everyone in the group.
- Periodically delete or archive data you no longer need — most groups are sitting on significant storage they've forgotten about.
- Keep a simple shared record of what data exists and where, so group members (and future group members) aren't starting from zero.
- For significant datasets, a brief data management plan saves time later and is often required by funders anyway.

### Choose infrastructure sensibly
- Shared institutional computing is usually more energy-efficient than running your own servers — and often cheaper once you factor in maintenance.
- If you use cloud, provider and region matter: some are considerably greener (and sometimes cheaper) than others.

### Know your footprint
- The [Green Algorithms calculator](https://www.green-algorithms.org) lets you estimate the carbon cost of individual analyses in a few minutes. Useful for grant applications and for identifying where the big wins are.

## Green DiSC accreditation

[Green DiSC](https://github.com/Cambridge-Sustainable-Computing-Lab/greenDiSC) is a sustainability accreditation for research groups. It gives a structured path to embedding good practices and formal recognition you can point to in grants, papers, and job applications.

There are two tiers:

- **Bronze** — foundational: data storage practices, awareness training, nominating a sustainability rep, and documenting your computing setup.
- **Silver** — builds on Bronze: estimating workflow carbon footprints, monitoring usage over time, and establishing a group policy.

The criteria overlap significantly with practices that make a group run better regardless of sustainability — so the accreditation process tends to be less onerous than it looks.
